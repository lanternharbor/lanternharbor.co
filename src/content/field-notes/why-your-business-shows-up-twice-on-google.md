Here is a small thing I run into often on the South Shore. An owner searches their own business on Google to make sure the hours are right, and two listings come back. Same shop, two cards. Maybe one says "Harbor Cafe" and the other says "Harbor Irish Pub." One has a nice batch of recent reviews. The other looks half-finished, has an old phone number, and is sitting on a lower star rating that nobody is watching.

If that is happening to you, you are in good company, and it is usually not your fault.

## How the duplicate got there

Most of the time it traces back to something ordinary. A previous owner set up a listing years ago and it never got cleaned up. A marketing vendor you hired once created a profile to "manage" it, then the relationship lapsed and the login went with them. Facebook auto-generated a page when someone checked in. You opened an Instagram account, then opened a second one when you forgot the password to the first.

None of these are dramatic. They just quietly stack up. Over a few years a business can end up scattered across Google, Yelp, TripAdvisor, Facebook, and Instagram, with small differences on each one. And the internet does not know that all those entries are the same place unless someone tells it.

## Why it is worth ten minutes of your attention

The reason this matters is simple. It makes you harder to find and a little harder to trust.

When your name shows up two different ways, a customer who heard about you from a friend types in one version and may not land on the version you actually keep updated. When an unclaimed listing is dragging around an old two-star average, that is often the first number a new customer sees, even though your real, current rating is much higher. When your followers are split between two Instagram handles, both of them look quieter than the real total.

Individually these are small. Together they add up to a business that looks a bit fuzzier online than it deserves to.

## The plain fix

The good news is that tidying this up is mostly patience, not technical wizardry. The path looks like this.

First, pick one canonical name. Decide, once, what you are called. "Harbor Cafe" or "Harbor Irish Pub," not both. This one decision drives everything else, so it is worth settling before you touch a single listing.

Second, claim and align each listing. Go platform by platform. Claim the ones you can prove you own, update each to the canonical name, correct the address, phone, and hours, and where a platform allows it, ask them to merge the duplicate into your real one. On Google this is a real, supported process, though it can take a little back-and-forth.

Third, consolidate your social accounts to one each. Aim for one Facebook page and one Instagram account, pointing at the same name and the same photos. Worth knowing up front: some platforms will not let you truly merge two accounts, so this is best effort toward one canonical each. Sometimes the honest move is to quietly retire the weaker account and point people to the one you keep.

When it is done, the payoff is not flashy. It is just that a customer who searches for you finds one clear, correct, current version of your business, with the good reviews attached to it. That is the whole goal.

## A low-pressure takeaway

You do not need to fix all of this today, and you do not need to hire anyone to start. The next time you have ten quiet minutes, search your own business name on Google, then on Facebook, then on Instagram, and just make a short list of what you find. Note every version of your name, every listing you did not remember creating, and every rating that surprised you.

That list is the entire job, written down. Once you can see the scattered pieces in one place, deciding on a single name and working through them one at a time is very doable. And if you would rather hand the untangling to someone else, that is exactly the kind of quiet, unglamorous cleanup I am happy to help a South Shore owner sort out.
