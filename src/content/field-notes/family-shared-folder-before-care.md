Most families I talk to on the South Shore go through some version of the same afternoon. A parent has a fall, or a hospital stay, or a diagnosis that changes things overnight, and suddenly everyone is texting each other trying to find basic information. What is Mom's Medicare number? Which pharmacy fills Dad's prescriptions? What is the login for the bank, the insurance, the online patient portal? Where is the list of what he actually takes each morning?

None of that is hard to find when life is calm. The trouble is that nobody goes looking until life is not calm, and by then the person who kept it all in their head is the one who needs help.

The good news is that this is one of the few parts of caregiving you can get ahead of. It does not take money or special software. It takes an ordinary afternoon and one shared, secured place to put things.

## What actually goes missing

When I ask families what they were scrambling for, the same short list comes up again and again:

- **Account logins.** Bank, retirement accounts, utilities, the phone bill, email. Often protected by a password only the parent knows.
- **Insurance and Medicare cards.** The physical cards, plus the plan details and member numbers.
- **Doctors and the pharmacy.** Names, phone numbers, and which office handles what.
- **A current medication list.** What they take, the dose, and why. This one changes over time, so a photo from two years ago is not enough.
- **Key documents.** Things like a health care proxy, power of attorney, or a will, if they exist, and where the originals live.
- **Emergency contacts.** The neighbor with a spare key, the sibling out of state, the longtime family doctor.

Any one of these is a phone call to track down. All of them at once, during a stressful week, is a genuine burden on top of an already hard moment.

## One folder, set up while things are calm

The fix is not complicated. You want a single place where a trusted family member can find this information, and you want it secured so it is not sitting in a plain email or a shoebox that could be lost.

A couple of practical ways to do it:

- **A shared cloud folder** in something the family already uses, with access limited to one or two trusted people. Scan or photograph the cards and documents, and keep a simple one-page document with the contacts and medication list.
- **A password manager** for the logins specifically. These are built to store passwords safely and to share access with a spouse or adult child. That keeps the sensitive credentials out of a document and behind real protection.

Many families use both. The point is less about the exact tool and more about having one agreed-upon place, secured, that the right people can reach.

A few things that make it work in practice. Put one person in charge of keeping it current, and pick a date once a year to check it, since medications and phone numbers drift. Make sure at least one other family member knows the folder exists and how to get in, because a folder only one person can open recreates the original problem. And have the conversation with your parent directly. Most are relieved to know the family will not be lost if something happens.

## A gentle nudge, not a project

This is general family preparedness, not medical or legal advice, and it does not need to be perfect to be worth doing. Even a rough version, half the list gathered into one secured folder, puts a family miles ahead of where most people start.

If you have been meaning to get organized for an aging parent, this is a good weekend to begin. Open one shared folder, add whatever you can find in an hour, and tell one other person it is there. You can fill in the rest over time. The work is small now and enormous later, and doing it before you need it is the whole point.
